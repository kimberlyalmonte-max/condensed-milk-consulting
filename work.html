What I've built

The operational problems that slow down mission-driven orgs tend to look a lot alike. Here's how I've solved them.

AI · Claude API The situation

Teachers were spending planning time decoding government standards documents that took expertise just to navigate.

What I built

Two AI chatbots that turn dense NYSED frameworks into plain-language answers. Collabby handles ELA standards, WIDA proficiency correlations, and bilingual resources for Spanish-speaking newcomers. The ALDS chatbot handles the Academic and Linguistic Demands framework, helping teachers understand the specific linguistic demands of individual standards. Both are fully WCAG-accessible and branded. Built on Claude with prompt caching, deployed on Netlify.

Teachers get answers in seconds instead of hunting through PDFs.
Google Apps Script · Salesforce The situation

Someone was manually creating sign-in sheets for every event — the morning of.

What I built

A Google Apps Script system that queries Salesforce every night for the next day's events, generates a formatted Excel spreadsheet and a branded print-ready PDF, then emails both to the right people automatically. Temporary Sheets are created for export and immediately trashed so nothing accumulates in Drive.

Zero manual sign-in sheet creation. Zero forgotten sheets. Zero morning scramble.
Salesforce Apex · Zoom API The situation

Zoom had the attendance data. Salesforce had the registrations. They never talked to each other.

What I built

A full Apex event management system: automated registration, cancellation, waitlist, reminder, post-event survey, and resource emails across two event programs, each with its own email template family. Real-time seat tracking rolled up to Opportunities. Zoom API integration that creates meetings, syncs who actually attended, and catches recording viewers after the fact via a nightly batch. Native ICS calendar invites and Google Calendar links attached to every registration and reminder email.

One source of truth for attendance. No manual emails, no manual cross-referencing, no forgotten Zoom links.
Salesforce Apex · Visualforce The situation

Event data lived in Salesforce but there was no way to see trends, compare years, or understand what was working.

What I built

A Visualforce dashboard with 8 analysis tabs: overview key metrics, by event type, by topic, by day of week, by time of day, by venue, by grade band, and by member role. Year-over-year comparison on every tab. Filters by full year, fall, or spring semester. Rate badges surface attendance trends at a glance.

Program staff can see what's working and report on impact — without pulling a single Salesforce report.
Salesforce Apex · Visualforce The situation

There was no way to quickly see which members needed attention — over seats, lapsed, or downgraded — without running multiple reports by hand.

What I built

A Visualforce dashboard showing the full membership picture: members over their seat allocation flagged with which programs they exceeded, lapsed and downgraded members with year-over-year comparison, and individual member detail views with seat utilization bars, note history, contact info, and event attendance. Includes a non-member prospects section showing organizations that attended events without a membership. Owner assignments are editable inline.

Staff can see who needs attention and act from one screen.
WordPress · Google Drive · Salesforce The situation

Members couldn't self-serve. Staff was fielding the same "can you send me that resource?" emails on repeat.

What I built

A WordPress plugin wiring together Google OAuth, Salesforce, and Google Drive. Members sign in with their school email, the system verifies their membership tier in Salesforce, and they browse a grade-band-filtered resource library pulling files directly from Google Drive — all without touching staff. Every login and download is logged via webhook.

Self-service for 280+ member schools. Staff spends less time answering the same requests.
WordPress · PHP The situation

Two complex educator workflows — ELL student identification and Regents transcript review — required navigating lengthy frameworks and doing checks by hand.

What I built

Two interactive tools inside the member portal. The ELL identification tool guides educators step-by-step through the identification process for individual students, referencing the appropriate NYSED criteria at each stage. The Regents transcript audit tool helps staff review student transcripts against graduation requirements interactively, flagging gaps automatically.

Complex compliance workflows that required expertise to navigate become guided processes anyone on the team can follow.
Salesforce Apex · FormAssembly · LWC The situation

Feedback from in-person professional development sessions arrived in a form and had to be manually sorted before it was usable.

What I built

FormAssembly connected to custom Salesforce objects. An Apex trigger automatically matches each submission to the correct parent session by school and date — creating the session record if it doesn't exist yet. A Lightning Web Component on the session record lets staff download all feedback as a formatted CSV with one click. Duplicate submissions from form reprocessing are handled via an external ID upsert.

Feedback arrives already organized. No manual sorting, no spreadsheet gymnastics.
Google Apps Script · Salesforce The situation

Each year, staff manually created a data collection spreadsheet for every school network — pulling campus data from Salesforce, pre-filling it, and emailing it out. Dozens of networks, done by hand.

What I built

A Google Apps Script system attached to an admin Google Sheet. Each row represents a network. A button pulls that network's campus data from Salesforce, generates a pre-filled spreadsheet, shares it with the listed contacts, and sends them the link — all in one step. Built to rerun safely: it checks for an existing sheet for the current school year before creating a new one.

What took hours of manual work each year now runs in seconds per network.
Google Apps Script · LearnWorlds API · Salesforce The situation

The learning platform had no idea which users were active members. Access was managed by hand.

What I built

A sync system running on three schedules: a daily light pass for same-day membership changes, a weekly sweep for new users, and a monthly full reconciliation. Domain-based lookup automatically matches users to their school's membership tier in Salesforce — no manual mapping required.

Membership changes in Salesforce reflect in the LMS within 24 hours, with no manual intervention.
Google Apps Script · Salesforce The situation

Google Groups membership had to be manually updated whenever someone's access changed in Salesforce.

What I built

A sync that keeps Google Groups membership aligned with Salesforce data automatically — no manual adds or removes needed when someone joins, leaves, or changes programs.

Group membership stays accurate without anyone manually managing it.
Salesforce Apex The situation

Years of data entry and staff turnover left a Salesforce org with duplicate contacts, bouncing addresses, and records linked to schools people had long since left.

What I built

A suite of scheduled data quality tools: a monthly duplicate merge batch with 7 matching rules (including fuzzy name matching and edit-distance typo detection on email domains); a nightly domain batch that catches email domain typos automatically and alerts on genuinely unknown domains; a twice-yearly MX record check that flags contacts at dead email domains before they bounce; and an inbound email service that parses forwarded bounce-back emails and marks the right contact automatically.

The contact database stays clean without manual audits. Bad email addresses get caught before they cause problems.
WordPress · Salesforce The situation

Events lived in Salesforce but the member portal had no calendar. Staff were maintaining two separate event lists to keep the website current.

What I built

A WordPress calendar that pulls event data directly from Salesforce. Events are entered once in Salesforce and appear on the member portal automatically — no second step, no copy-pasting, no risk of the two getting out of sync.

One place to manage events. The website stays current automatically.
Google Apps Script · LearnWorlds API The situation

LearnWorlds shows individual user progress but has no way to see course-level data — completion rates, average time spent, how many people even started.

What I built

A Google Apps Script that pulls all user progress data from the LearnWorlds API into a structured Google Sheet, with a summary tab showing completion rates and average and median time spent per course. Handles rate limiting, pagination, and large datasets automatically so it can be rerun any time without manual intervention.

Course performance data that the LMS doesn't surface is now in a spreadsheet anyone on the team can read.
WordPress · Google Sheets API The situation

Two curated content collections on the member portal required a developer to update — a bookshelf and an educator voices feed.

What I built

Two WordPress features driven entirely by Google Sheets. Staff update a spreadsheet; the website updates automatically. No WordPress login, no developer needed. The bookshelf organizes curated books by identity and representation category. The educator voices feed displays article cards with author info and photos. Both are cached to keep load times fast.

Non-technical staff maintain both content collections independently, without touching the website.
