What I've built

Apps Script · Salesforce The situation

Someone was manually creating sign-in sheets for every event — the morning of.

What I built

A Google Apps Script system that queries Salesforce every night for the next day's events, generates a formatted Excel spreadsheet and a branded print-ready PDF, then emails both to the right people automatically. Temporary Sheets are created for export and immediately trashed so nothing accumulates in Drive.

The result

Zero manual sign-in sheet creation. Zero forgotten sheets. Zero morning scramble.

Salesforce · Zoom The situation

Zoom had the attendance data. Salesforce had the registrations. They never talked to each other.

What I built

A full Apex event management system: automated registration, cancellation, waitlist, reminder, post-event survey, and resource emails across two event programs, each with its own email template family. Real-time seat tracking rolled up to Opportunities. Zoom API integration that creates meetings, syncs who actually attended, and catches recording viewers after the fact via a nightly batch. Native ICS calendar invites and Google Calendar links attached to every registration and reminder email.

The result

One source of truth for attendance. No manual emails, no manual cross-referencing, no forgotten Zoom links.

WordPress · Google Drive · Salesforce The situation

Members couldn't self-serve. Staff was fielding the same "can you send me that resource?" emails on repeat.

What I built

A WordPress plugin wiring together Google OAuth, Salesforce, and Google Drive. Members sign in with their school email, the system verifies their membership tier in Salesforce, and they browse a grade-band-filtered resource library pulling files directly from Google Drive — all without touching staff. Every login and download is logged via webhook.

The result

Self-service for 280+ member schools. Staff spends less time answering the same requests.

AI · Claude The situation

Teachers were spending planning time decoding government standards documents that took expertise just to navigate.

What I built

Two AI chatbots that turn dense NYSED frameworks into plain-language answers. Collabby handles ELA standards, WIDA proficiency correlations, and bilingual resources for Spanish-speaking newcomers. The ALDS chatbot handles the Academic and Linguistic Demands framework, helping teachers understand the specific linguistic demands of individual standards. Both are fully WCAG-accessible and branded. Built on Claude with prompt caching, deployed on Netlify.

The result

Teachers get answers in seconds instead of hunting through PDFs.

Apps Script · Salesforce The situation

Each year, staff manually created a data collection spreadsheet for every school network — pre-filling it with campus data from Salesforce and emailing it to the right contacts. Dozens of networks, done by hand.

What I built

A Google Apps Script system attached to an admin Google Sheet. Each row represents a network. A button pulls that network's campus data from Salesforce, generates a pre-filled spreadsheet, shares it with the listed contacts, and sends them the link — all in one step. Built to rerun safely: it checks for an existing sheet before creating a new one.

The result

What took hours of manual work each year now runs in seconds per network.

Apps Script · LearnWorlds · Salesforce The situation

The learning platform had no idea which users were active members. Access was managed by hand.

What I built

A sync system running on three schedules: a daily light pass for same-day membership changes, a weekly sweep for new users, and a monthly full reconciliation. Domain-based lookup automatically matches users to their school's membership tier in Salesforce — no manual mapping required.

Membership changes in Salesforce reflect in the LMS within 24 hours, with no manual intervention.

Salesforce · FormAssembly The situation

Feedback from in-person professional development sessions arrived in a form and had to be manually sorted before it was usable.

What I built

FormAssembly connected to custom Salesforce objects. An Apex trigger automatically matches each submission to the correct parent session by school and date — creating the session record if it doesn't exist yet. A Lightning Web Component on the session record lets staff download all feedback as a formatted CSV with one click. Duplicate submissions are handled via an external ID upsert.

The result

Feedback arrives already organized. No manual sorting, no spreadsheet gymnastics.

Salesforce The situation

Years of data entry and staff turnover left a Salesforce org with duplicate contacts, bouncing addresses, and records linked to schools people had long since left.

What I built

A suite of scheduled data quality tools: a monthly duplicate merge batch with 7 matching rules (including fuzzy name matching and edit-distance typo detection on email domains); a nightly domain batch that catches email domain typos and alerts on unknown domains; a twice-yearly MX record check that flags contacts at dead email domains before they bounce; and an inbound email service that parses forwarded bounce-back emails and marks the right contact automatically.

The result

The contact database stays clean without manual audits. Bad email addresses get caught before they cause problems.
